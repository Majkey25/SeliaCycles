# Daily guidance and fertility estimates

## Recorded dates take precedence

`CycleInsights.fertilityEstimates` previously replaced estimates only with recorded starts after the reference date. Historical fertility could therefore remain tied to an old forecast after the period was corrected.

The live fertility view now uses recorded starts in every month. It uses the existing forecast-to-record matching rule to suppress the corresponding old fertility window. Saved period forecasts remain unchanged for comparison. A recorded period start does not confirm an ovulation date: the app still subtracts the configured luteal interval.

Cycle length is elapsed days between period starts. No arbitrary one-day adjustment was added to match another app. Tests cover leap years, month boundaries, and year boundaries. The luteal setting now states its counting convention and limitations.

## Guidance follows the selected day

The menstrual guide has early, middle, and later reading groups. These are editorial groups, not measured hormonal subphases: days 1–2 are early; the final two expected days, starting no earlier than day 3, are later. Remaining days are middle. Confirmed episode length takes precedence over the usual duration. After a recorded end, phase guidance no longer extends menstruation merely because the average is longer.

Personal menstrual mood summaries use confirmed bleeding observations within one day of the selected relative day, across at least two completed cycles and three observations. This is a description of logged experience, not a validated mood prediction. Pregnancy and menopause do not receive ordinary menstrual-phase guidance.

## Evidence and limits

Sources checked on 19 September 2026:

- [NHS: Period pain](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/period-pain/) supports discussion of pain near the start, wrapped warmth, gentle movement, and warning signs. It does not establish a fixed symptom schedule for every person.
- [NHS: PMS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pre-menstrual-syndrome/) describes varied symptoms and recommends tracking experience. Regular meals, sleep, and activity are general care options, not phase-specific treatment guarantees.
- [Office on Women's Health: Menstrual cycle and health](https://womenshealth.gov/menstrual-cycle/your-menstrual-cycle-and-your-health) provides patient education linked from the expanded guide.
- [Pletzer et al., 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44294-026-00140-z) studied stress vulnerability and sociability in 68 adult participants over 75 days. Group-level findings do not predict an individual's personality, relationships, or daily emotions and must not be generalized to children.
- [ASRM: Optimizing natural fertility](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/optimizing-natural-fertility-a-committee-opinion-2021/) explains the variability and limits of calendar estimates. Matching another calendar app is not evidence of clinical accuracy.

The app does not promise safe days, pregnancy probabilities, confirmed ovulation, a special cycle diet, or supplement benefits. Severe or worsening pain, persistent symptoms, and unusually heavy or prolonged bleeding warrant medical advice. Source links open static public URLs without sending profile or health data.
